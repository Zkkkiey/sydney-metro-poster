Extra analysis · not on the poster

Cost rise after adjusting for inflation (indicative)

The poster compares the 2016 and December 2025 cost estimates in nominal dollars, a rise of 76–100% [1, p. 19]. Part of this rise is only general price growth. As a reference, we used the ABS Consumer Price Index (CPI) to convert the 2016 estimate into 2025 prices.

+76–100%Nominal rise (on the poster)
+33–51%After removing inflation

How we calculated it

  1. CPI, June quarter 2016
    8 capital cities, 2011–12 = 100 [27]    108.6
  2. CPI, September quarter 2025
    same base [28]    143.6
  3. Price growth
    (143.6 − 108.6) ÷ 108.6 [29]    32.2%
  4. 2016 estimate in 2025 prices
    $11.5–12.5bn × 1.322    $15.2–16.5bn
  5. December 2025 estimate
    [1, p. 19]    $22–23bn
Cost rise = (actual cost − estimated cost) ÷ estimated cost [30]
Low end: (22 − 16.5) ÷ 16.5 ≈ 33%
High end: (23 − 15.2) ÷ 15.2 ≈ 51%

Why this is only a reference

2016 basisWe do not know whether the 2016 estimate already included future price growth.
Wrong index?CPI measures household prices, not construction costs.
Added scopeThe figures still include added work, such as Central Walk.
Latest CPIWe used September 2025 CPI because later quarters are published on a new base.

So even after removing inflation, the cost still rose clearly, but we still cannot call the whole rise an overrun.
